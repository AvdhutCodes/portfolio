> [!quote] The Philosophy of In-Yun
> "There is a word in Korean: In-Yun. It means providence or fate. It’s an In-Yun if even two strangers just walk by each other in the street and their clothes accidentally brush."

## The Quiet Dignity of Adult Love

In almost any Hollywood romance, the arrival of Hae Sung in New York City would lead to cheap melodrama: a jealous husband, a screaming argument in a bar, a dramatic kiss in the torrential rain.

Celine Song rejects every cliché.

Arthur (John Magaro), the Jewish American writer married to Nora, is not an obstacle to be overcome. He is kind, perceptive, and heartbreakingly honest about his insecurities: *"You make the world so much bigger and I'm wondering if I'm just the guy you met because you were living in this place."*

## The Final Walk to the Uber

The film lives or dies in its final three minutes:
- Nora walks Hae Sung to the street corner.
- They stand in silence for two full minutes while traffic glides by.
- Hae Sung looks at her: *"See you then."*
- Nora walks back up the apartment steps, collapses into Arthur’s arms, and weeps for the girl she left in Seoul twenty-four years ago.

A masterpiece of restraint and mature emotional truth.
