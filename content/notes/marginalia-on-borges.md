> "I have always imagined that Paradise will be a kind of library."

## The Hexagonal Universe

In *The Library of Babel*, Jorge Luis Borges constructs a universe composed of an indefinite, perhaps infinite number of hexagonal galleries. In each hexagon there are shelves; on each shelf are thirty-two books of uniform size; each book has four hundred and ten pages; each page, forty lines; each line, eighty letters.

Because the library contains every possible permutation of the alphabet, it holds everything:
- The detailed history of the future.
- The lost tragedies of Aeschylus.
- The accurate translation of every dead language.
- The true story of your own death.

And along with those, millions of volumes of pure nonsensical clutter: lines like *“dhcmrlchtdj”* repeating for five hundred leaves.

## The Modern Internet as the Library of Babel

We now live inside Borges' library. The internet is that exact infinite hexagon. Every theory, every opinion, every contradictory gospel is accessible in a microsecond.

The challenge of our era is no longer finding information. **It is curation, discernment, and subtraction.**

When every book is available, the true art is choosing which five to keep on your bedside table.
