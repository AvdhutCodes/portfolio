## The Foundational Shelf

Here are the ten works I return to when the noise of modern life threatens to dissolve critical thought:

### 1. *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* — Eric Jorgenson
The definitive handbook on wealth, judgment, leverage, and unconditional happiness. It reads like contemporary Marcus Aurelius mixed with Silicon Valley game theory.

### 2. *Meditations* — Marcus Aurelius (Gregory Hays translation)
The ultimate armor against anxiety and status anxiety. Read a single paragraph each evening.

### 3. *Poor Charlie’s Almanack* — Charlie Munger & Peter Kaufman
The masterclass on worldly wisdom, multi-disciplinary thinking, and psychological misjudgments.

### 4. *Antifragile* — Nassim Nicholas Taleb
Things that gain from disorder. Essential for understanding risk, asymmetry, and modern fragility.

### 5. *Ficciones* — Jorge Luis Borges
Labyrinths, infinite libraries, and the paradox of time. Expands the architectural limits of human imagination.

### 6. *The Beginning of Infinity* — David Deutsch
A physicist's defense of human explanation and open-ended progress. The best antidote to contemporary cynicism.

### 7. *Essays* — Michel de Montaigne
The birth of honest introspection. Montaigne wrote candidly about his flaws, his kidney stones, and his love of quiet books.

### 8. *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance* — Robert Pirsig
An inquiry into Quality and the reconciliation of classical rationality with romantic intuition.

### 9. *Letters from a Stoic* — Seneca
Timeless advice on friendship, time management, and the brevity of life.

### 10. *Gödel, Escher, Bach* — Douglas Hofstadter
A golden braid connecting formal logic, recursive fugues, and the mystery of consciousness.
