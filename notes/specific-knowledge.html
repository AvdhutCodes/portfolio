The Architecture of Specific Knowledge

Why you cannot be trained for what makes you genuinely irreplaceable

By Avdhut Kulkarni · 2026-03-14 · 3 min read
#Leverage#Navalism#Mental Models#Career
Naval's Axiom

"Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your genuine curiosity and passion rather than whatever is hot right now. If you're not 100% into it, somebody else who is 100% into it will outperform you."

The Mirage of Transferable Credentials

In modern academic and corporate life, we are trained to seek transferable credentials: standardized degrees, certifications, and universal checklists. But anything that can be taught in a curriculum can also be outsourced, automated, or taught to a million competitors.

Specific knowledge is different. It is found much more by pursuing your innate curiosity and obsession than by asking: what does the market want right now?

When you pursue what feels like play to you, but looks like work to others, you create a natural moat. Nobody can compete with you on being you.

The Three Hallmarks of Specific Knowledge

  1. It is acquired through apprenticeship and curiosity, not classroom lectures. You cannot read a textbook to learn judgment in chaotic environments. You learn it by making bets, taking scars, and watching masters at work.
  2. It often appears weird or trivial at first. Think of early computer hobbyists tinkering in garages in 1975, or obsessively cataloging obscure Latin translations in a monastery. What the world later discovers as immense leverage often begins as a solitary obsession.
  3. It operates with high accountability. You cannot have specific knowledge without putting your name on the line. When you take accountability, you take the downside risks, but you gain the upside of equity and reputation.
Marginal Note from Obsidian

Compare this with permissionless leverage and the judgment trade. When specific knowledge meets scalable code and media, output is disconnected from input.

The Test of Play vs. Work

Ask yourself:

  • What did you do between ages 12 and 18 when nobody told you what to study?
  • What books do you read that you never feel compelled to skim?
  • What problem keeps you awake not out of anxiety, but out of fascination?

Specific knowledge cannot be taught, but it can be learned. If they can train you for it, they will eventually train an AI or hire someone at half the price to replace you. Guard your eccentricities.
