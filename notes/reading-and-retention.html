The Bibliophile's Guide to Reading and Forgetting

You do not remember the meals that kept you alive, yet they formed your bones

Emerson's Observation

"I cannot remember the books I've read any more than the meals I have eaten; even so, they have made me."

The Vanity of the Reading Goal

Every January, intelligent people set an arbitrary number: I will read 52 books this year.

This metric rewards brevity, shallowness, and speed-reading over deep comprehension. If you read 52 airport business books, you have absorbed roughly twelve recycled tweets padded into 250 pages each.

If instead you spent an entire year wrestling with The Enchiridion of Epictetus, Montaigne's Essays, and Jorge Luis Borges' Ficciones, your year would be infinitely richer.

How to Read Like a Bibliophile

1. Ruthless Abandonment

The moment a book bores you after page thirty, shut it. Put it down without guilt. Life is too short and there are too many magnificent books written over three thousand years to waste time on bad prose.

2. Reread the Top 1%

The best books are not meant to be read once; they are meant to be lived with.

  • When you reread a book at 20, 30, and 40, the book hasn't changed—you have.
  • Naval says: "I would rather read the best 100 books over and over again until I absorb them completely than read all the books."

3. Read with a Pen in Hand

A book that is clean is a book that was merely visited, not inhabited. Underline, write marginalia in the gutters, argue with the author in pencil. Turn the physical artifact into a dialog between two minds.

Obsidian Workflow Note

When an idea hits you while reading, don't summarize the chapter. Capture your own reaction in your vault. Link it to marginalia on borges and books that shaped my mind.

The Ultimate Filter

Read what you love until you love to read. Then read the originals, not the commentary on the originals.
